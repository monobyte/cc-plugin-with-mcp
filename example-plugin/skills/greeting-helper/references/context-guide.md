# Greeting Context Selection Guide

Detailed guidance on selecting the right greeting style for any situation.

## Decision Framework

Use this framework to quickly determine the appropriate greeting style:

```
Is this communication...
│
├── Official/External?
│   └── Use FORMAL
│
├── Internal/Team-based?
│   │
│   ├── Routine update?
│   │   └── Use CASUAL
│   │
│   ├── Celebration/Welcome?
│   │   └── Use ENTHUSIASTIC
│   │
│   └── Unknown context?
│       └── Start CASUAL, adjust as needed
│
└── First interaction?
    └── Use FORMAL, then match their tone
```

## Detailed Scenarios

### When to Use Formal Greetings

**Always use formal greetings for:**

1. **External communications**
   - Client emails
   - Vendor correspondence
   - Partner organizations
   - Regulatory bodies

2. **Senior leadership**
   - C-suite communications
   - Board presentations
   - Executive updates
   - Investor relations

3. **Legal and HR matters**
   - Contracts and agreements
   - Performance documentation
   - Compliance communications
   - Official notices

4. **First-time interactions**
   - Initial outreach
   - Job interviews
   - New client meetings
   - Conference introductions

### When to Use Casual Greetings

**Casual greetings work best for:**

1. **Team communications**
   - Daily standups
   - Slack messages
   - Internal project updates
   - Team brainstorming

2. **Established relationships**
   - Regular collaborators
   - Repeat clients (once rapport built)
   - Long-term partners
   - Familiar vendors

3. **Informal settings**
   - Team chat channels
   - Quick questions
   - Casual catch-ups
   - Internal feedback

4. **Peer-to-peer**
   - Same-level colleagues
   - Cross-team collaboration
   - Working groups
   - Study groups

### When to Use Enthusiastic Greetings

**Enthusiastic greetings are perfect for:**

1. **Celebrations**
   - Achievement announcements
   - Milestone completions
   - Award recognitions
   - Promotion news

2. **Welcomes**
   - New team members
   - Returning colleagues
   - Guest speakers
   - Event attendees

3. **Kickoffs**
   - Project launches
   - Initiative beginnings
   - Sprint starts
   - Campaign launches

4. **Motivation**
   - Team rallies
   - Challenging periods
   - Important deadlines
   - Competitive moments

## Tone Indicators

### Signs to Go More Formal

- Recipient uses titles (Dr., Mr., Ms.)
- Communication chain includes leadership
- Topic involves contracts or legal matters
- Recipient's messages are formal
- You're uncertain about the relationship
- Cultural context suggests formality

### Signs to Go More Casual

- Recipient uses first names
- You've exchanged multiple messages
- Topic is routine or operational
- Team culture is relaxed
- Recipient responds casually
- Internal team communication

### Signs to Go Enthusiastic

- Good news to share
- Team morale opportunity
- Welcoming someone new
- Celebrating success
- Building excitement
- Positive team building

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Too Formal

- Using formal tone in casual team chats
- Maintaining formality after established rapport
- Over-using titles with peers
- Stiff language in celebratory moments

### Too Casual

- First emails to clients being too informal
- Skipping formality with senior executives
- Casual tone in official documentation
- Informal language in multi-stakeholder threads

### Too Enthusiastic

- Excessive enthusiasm in routine updates
- Celebratory tone during serious discussions
- Over-the-top welcomes that feel insincere
- Forced enthusiasm that doesn't match content

## Adaptation Strategies

### Reading the Room

1. **Check previous messages**: How did they communicate?
2. **Consider the channel**: Email vs chat vs meeting
3. **Note the audience**: Who else is involved?
4. **Mind the timing**: Urgent vs casual follow-up

### Adjusting Mid-Conversation

- If they're more casual: Relax your tone slightly
- If they're more formal: Elevate your professionalism
- If the mood shifts: Adapt accordingly
- When in doubt: Err on the side of formality
